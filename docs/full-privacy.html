SoundsGood Full Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 25, 2026

Publisher: Akeem Oluwadamilare Sulaimon. For privacy questions or requests, email thedream.akeems@gmail.com.

This policy describes SoundsGood Full for Android and its optional SoundsGood Sync Mac companion. SoundsGood Full is a personal music manager that processes your library on your devices. It does not require a developer account. The reviewed application has no developer-operated cloud collection endpoint and no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting SDK. This does not mean that the app processes no personal information: your listening history, annotations and other library information can be personal.

Information used on your Android device

With your permission, the app reads music from device storage and folders you select. It uses audio metadata such as titles, artists, albums, duration, genre and embedded artwork to display and play your library. Its private local database stores library records, listening events and counts, playback state, playlists, mix rules, favorites and other preferences, moods and journal entries.

The app also keeps selected-folder settings and the most recent sync result report. That report contains library, track, playlist and report identifiers, the library revision, transfer byte counts, verification results and fixed status or error codes. The report format does not include pairing credentials, source paths or song titles.

Transfers that you initiate

Local Wi-Fi sync transfers selected music, playlists and related metadata from your Mac to Android. The Android sync client accepts private local IPv4 addresses and uses encrypted TLS with the certificate fingerprint supplied in the pairing code. It sends requests and, when the session permits, a terminal transfer result report directly to the paired Mac. Reports can describe successful transfers as well as failures. These are transfers between your devices, rather than uploads to a SoundsGood cloud service.

Pairing codes grant access to the selected Mac library while that sharing session is active. Keep them private. Android holds the pairing credentials in memory for the session; disconnecting forgets them. Stopping sharing on the Mac invalidates the old pairing code. The Mac creates temporary TLS credentials for the session, using memory where supported or an isolated temporary keychain, with best-effort cleanup when that session identity is released; this is not a guarantee of secure erasure.

The Mac companion can also export selected music and metadata to a transfer folder for USB or other user-managed copying. The Android app imports from the folder you select.

Backups, saved reports and storage providers

Full's manual library backup is readable, unencrypted JSON. It can contain private journal text, moods, preferences, playlists, custom mix rules, unmatched records, track references and an installation identifier. Track references can include titles, artists, albums, filenames and content hashes. This metadata backup is not a backup of the audio files themselves. Choose its destination carefully and protect copies you keep or share.

You can also save the last Android sync report through the system document picker. The Mac's Save a sync report on Desktop option is enabled by default and can be turned off before sharing. Mac Desktop reports can include song titles and artists, timestamps, skipped entries and transfer outcomes. They do not include source paths, request headers or pairing credentials.

Android document providers and your Mac's folder settings may involve another service. For example, a chosen document provider may upload an exported backup, or iCloud Desktop synchronization may upload a Mac report. Those services operate under their own settings and privacy policies. SoundsGood does not guarantee that a location selected through a system picker is physically local or excluded from cloud synchronization.

A managed Music folder can also contain recovery records with library and track metadata, integrity hashes, and playlist names and order. These shared records survive uninstall and are accessible to apps with permission to the folder.

Permissions

  • Audio/storage access lets the app find and play music. Folder grants provide access to the particular source or destination you choose; managed sync destinations need writing access.
  • Notifications provide playback controls and sync progress or results.
  • Foreground playback and connected-device services support ongoing playback and user-started local sync.
  • Internet, network-state and network-change permissions support the local connection. A wake lock keeps the CPU available during active sync.

You can manage permissions in Android settings. Revoking access may make the relevant music or transfer feature unavailable.

Retention and deletion

Private app information remains on your device until you remove relevant records using available controls, clear the app's storage, or uninstall it. The most recent private sync report is replaced by a later report. The application configuration excludes its private data from Android automatic backup and device transfer.

Clearing or uninstalling the app does not necessarily delete music in a user-selected folder, exported backups, USB copies or saved Desktop reports. Manage those files separately using the relevant file manager or storage provider. Stopping a sharing session does not delete reports already saved.

Support, store and website

If you contact the publisher, the information you choose to send, including your email address and message or attachments, is used to answer your request and troubleshoot the issue. Support correspondence is retained only as needed for those purposes and any applicable legal obligations. You may request deletion by emailing the contact above. Please do not send pairing codes, passwords, signing keys, private journals or music files unless a specific support need has first been discussed.

Google Play handles purchases and distribution under its own terms and privacy policy. This policy does not describe Google's independent processing. These documentation pages are hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may process technical visit information, such as IP addresses and request details, under GitHub's privacy statement. These pages add no analytics scripts or advertising trackers.
